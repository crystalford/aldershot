Directing

Give it a direction. Go and do something else.

Talk to it the way you’d talk to an editor. It answers with a plan — the steps, the shots each one touches, what it costs against your limit — and when you say go, the crew works through it while you keep going. When it’s done: a reel of what changed, the few things that need your eye, and what it would do next.

Scene by scene, one small thing at a time

Twenty minutes a sitting. Whole scenes, finished.

A twenty-minute essay isn’t made in a sitting — it’s made in a week, and you can watch it the whole time. Each scene moves through five steps, and only the next one asks anything of you. Today’s sitting picks the scenes you can finish in about twenty minutes, so you stop on something done.

Every shot is a choice

It starts as a sketch. Make it whatever it needs to be.

Click any shot and you see the one Aldershot recommends, and why. Keep it drawn. Have Scout find the real thing. Use your own. Or generate from the sketch — it becomes the layout the picture follows, drawn or painted, never photoreal. Over a claim, a generated picture is labelled a depiction, and the claim keeps its receipt.

Set what the pictures may cost once, for the whole film — nothing, a little, or where it shows — and every recommendation follows it.

Scout

Your footage is the floor. Scout raises it.

Scout looks for what the film is missing — the proof behind a claim, the other side, the original a clip came from, someone else making the same point — in NASA’s library, the public archives, podcasts, the news and your own recordings. It reads what it finds before it shows you, links it at the right second, and checks whether it can be used. It never downloads anything a platform forbids.

Pricing

Pay for what it reads. Editing is free.
