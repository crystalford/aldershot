THE PLAN

A film is a weave. Your voice, their words, room to breathe.

Documentary editors build the sound first and cover it with pictures after. Aldershot’s plan works the same way: acts, then sections that each raise or answer a question, then a two-column script — picture on the left, sound on the right. Your lines arrive as prompts to record, sized to the time they have.

THE CREW

You direct. The crew does the legwork — and asks before anything changes.

ALDERSHOT
Holds the structure — the argument, the acts, the length. When the material pulls the story somewhere new, it says so, once, with what it would cost: “your stream and your video disagree — that might be the essay.”
SCOUT
Finds what your footage is missing — the proof, the other side, the original. He links to it at the right second and never downloads from places that forbid it. What he can’t reach comes back to you with a QR code.
SKETCH
Draws what can be drawn — orbits, charts, maps, the path a signal took — in code, from real numbers, timed to your line. Yours to approve. Good enough to keep more often than you’d think.
YOU
One small thing at a time, each one saying how long it takes and what it gets you. Talk over the film while it plays — every note lands at the moment you said it.
THE DRAWN FINISH
No footage yet? Make it Drawn.

Any film can be finished as motion graphics and your voice alone — a sketch to build on, or the cut you post. Anything standing in for a real object says so on screen, and a recreation can never stand in for a proof.
