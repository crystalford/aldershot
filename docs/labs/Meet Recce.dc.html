Recce appears only when Scout is looking beyond what you've uploaded. He shows one thing — the state of the search — and says it in the product's voice. He finds and links; he never downloads, never cheers, and never has an opinion about anyone. He's a scout probe — a pale pod that faces forward, a dark visor with one lens, two swept fins, a thin sensor mast with a light, a thruster at the back — because a scout is the thing you send out past your own footage. The shell is pale so he reads on the dark interface. He carries one colour at a time, and it says what he's doing: teal while he's out looking, green when a find verifies, gold when there's a card for you to grab, red when he's come back with nothing. The same meanings those colours have on every other screen.

WHAT HE'S MADE OF
01The silhouette. A forward-facing pod with a visor, two fins swept back, a mast, a flame behind — a small craft going somewhere, readable in black at 18px, pale enough to stand off the dark UI at a glance. A probe, because recce is reconnaissance: you send it out to see past your own footage.
02One eye, one colour at a time. The lens, the mast light, the flame and the glow beneath all take the same colour, and the colour is the state: teal out looking, green verified, gold a mission, red thin, grey off. Never two colours at once — that was the version that read as a badge. A white catchlight keeps the eye alive; it blinks while he waits and scans side to side while he looks.
03Motion says the rest. Idle is a slow hover and a short flame. Looking is flight — a lean forward, a long flickering flame, the lens scanning with the searchlight sweeping ahead, two speed lines behind. Thin is a tilt down and a weak flame. A mission is the card slung underneath. Off is grounded: shell dulled, no flame, lens dark.
04The tick. On a verified find the colour turns green and a small tick appears inside the lens — under half a second. No confetti, no sound.
05Why this and not the last one. The first drafts carried the wordmark's bars and then four colours; both read as badges, and colour fields fight a dark UI at small sizes. The redesign follows the rules every good small character follows: silhouette first, one accent, the eye as the focal point, and state carried by motion.
HOW HE BEHAVES
He only shows up when looking outside is on. Inside your own footage the system speaks as itself; Recce is the part of it that went out.
He shows state, not personality. Six states, nothing else. He has no face when it's bad news — a thin result is a dimmed lens and a flat line, not a frown.
He never celebrates. A find is a tick and a sentence with a timestamp. "Verified against the source at 12:34", not "Nice catch!".
He never has an opinion about a person. He reports behaviour — what was said, on camera, when — and brings the other side with it.
He finds and links. When something has to be grabbed, he hands you the card. He doesn't go and get it.

WHY A CHARACTER AT ALL, IN A PRODUCT THAT BANS MASCOTS
Meta's Muse found it in user research: long conversations with an agent that had no face or personality felt like talking to a corporate logo — so Muse asks you to name yours, and people respond best to the one they named. The same holds here, narrowly: looking outside is the one thing the system does that isn't about your footage, and the user should feel the difference. So he has a shape, and a name you choose. Recce marks the boundary: inside, the system speaks as itself; outside, something went and came back with a report. He's an indicator with a shape, not a friend — the design-language rule stands: he never celebrates, never decorates, and never appears where nothing was searched.
